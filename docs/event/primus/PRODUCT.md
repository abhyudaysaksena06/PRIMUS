# PRIMUS for Product teams, explained simply

**You run a startup.** Your company has shares, like a pizza cut into 100,000 slices. You keep 60,000. The better
your company does, the more each slice is worth. That is your score: **your slices × the final price + your money.**

## Your sector

At 21:00 the lottery puts you in a squad (you, a consulting firm and an investment fund) and gives your squad a
**sector**: FMCG, Aviation or Luxury. Everything you do tonight comes from that sector. You'll see it as a coloured
badge at the top of every page.

## The three rounds

1. **Round 1 · Build (21:10–22:00).** Write your **PRD**: your startup's plan, in seven short parts, up to 800
   words. A robot judge marks it out of 100. Good marks = a higher starting price. Then answer investors' questions.
2. **Round 2 · Four crises (22:45–04:45).** Four times a **crisis** strikes: every price falls 5% and your company
   is dealt a crisis card. The card stays **sealed until hiring closes**, so you hire blind. Each time:
   - **hire a consulting firm** in the first 20 minutes (offer them money or slices; they can say yes, no or ask
     for more) — judge them on their record and their pitch, because nobody yet knows what the crisis is. Each firm
     has a **minimum bid**, its base rate: 2% of your company, plus 0.04% for every point its Round 1 memo scored
     above 50 (minus 0.04% for every point below), never below 1% or above 4%. If you hire no one, you are given a
     **random firm** and pay that firm's base rate;
   - **read your crisis card**, which opens to everyone the moment hiring closes;
   - **write your response plan** (up to 500 words) before the due time, using your firm's advice;
   - **rate your firm** 1 to 5 stars.
   A good plan pushes your price up; a weak or missing one pushes it down. During Round 2 you can also **raise
   money**: sell 1% to 10% of your company to a fund at the market price, once per crisis. You always keep at least
   40,000 of your shares (40%).
3. **Round 3 · The final problem (04:45–06:30).** In the **draft** you pick a problem statement from your sector,
   lowest score so far first, 20 seconds each. The three sectors draft at the same time, each in its own queue. Miss
   your turn and you get a random statement of your sector that still has room. Then hire a firm and write your
   **solution** together. Your solution's score moves your price, and Round 3 evens out the field: the weakest
   company going into Round 3 gets ×1.25 of its price rise, the strongest ×0.25, and everyone else in between
   ("score so far" is the average of your PRD and your four crisis plans). A fall applies in full.

## Writing tonight

- **Type everything.** Paste is turned off in the writing boxes, and a draft that grows faster than anyone can
  type is flagged to the organisers.
- **Your draft saves itself.** Submit opens only in the last 5 minutes before the deadline. At the deadline the
  last saved version is submitted for you. If you press Submit and then change your text, press Submit again.
- **Stay within the word limit.** A draft over the limit cannot be submitted, not even at the deadline. Your PRD
  also needs a company name and a free 3–4 letter ticker.

## Tips

- Your Home page says **"Do this now"** with a countdown. Do that.
- The step row at the top shows where you are in each crisis.
- When a crisis arrives your card shows up big on screen. Read it carefully: your plan must answer it.
- **One screen per team.** Extra phones or laptops still work, but they update more slowly.
- The **Leaderboard** shows every company's **PRIMUS Rating**. Everyone starts at 1,000. Each released PRD, crisis
  plan and solution moves it, by how your score compares with the other companies in your sector that round (at
  most 100 points up or down per round).
