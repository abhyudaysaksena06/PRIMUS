# PRIMUS for Consulting firms, explained simply

**You are a firm of advisers.** Companies pay you to help them through their crises. Your score is the money you
earn: **fees + bonuses + the value of any shares you were paid in.**

## How you earn

1. **Round 1 · Prove yourself (21:10–22:00).** Every firm writes a memo on the same case (ZestUp). A good memo earns
   a bonus and builds your name: its score also sets your **base rate**, the minimum bid a company must offer you in
   every crisis. The base rate is 2% of the client company, plus 0.04% for every point your score is above 50
   (minus 0.04% for every point below), never below 1% or above 4%. The score is the **average of your Round 1 memo
   and every advice memo score released so far**; it is recalculated after each crisis's advice scores are released.
   An average of 75 makes it 3%; 100 makes it 4%; with no score released yet it is 2%.
   Then, by 22:30, make **Call 1**: for each of the two companies you follow, guess whether its price will go up
   (BUY) or down (SELL) from its IPO price to the end of trading round 4. Each correct call earns $2,500.
2. **Round 2 · Win clients (22:45–04:45).** In each of the four crises:
   - companies send you **offers**: money, shares or both, at least your base rate. Accept, decline or ask for
     more. You can take **two clients** per crisis. A company that hires no one is given a random firm with room,
     at that firm's base rate, so you may also be **assigned** a client;
   - write an **advice memo** for each client before the due time: **at least 150 words** (up to 500). **No memo,
     or a memo under 150 words, = no fee**: it goes back to the client;
   - your clients **rate you** 1 to 5 stars. Good memos and good stars earn a bonus; poor ones cost you.
3. **Round 3 · The last job (04:45–06:30).** First the companies' draft: the 10 lowest-scoring companies in each
   sector pick a statement, lowest first, 20 seconds each, each statement once; every other company in the sector is
   given a statement at random, spread evenly across the ten. Then get hired again (hiring opens when the draft ends,
   about 04:50, until 05:15) and help your client write its solution.

## Writing tonight

- **Type everything.** Paste is turned off in the writing boxes, copying text from the site is turned off too, and
  a draft that grows faster than anyone can type is flagged to the organisers.
- **Your draft saves itself.** Submit opens only in the last 5 minutes before the deadline. At the deadline the
  last saved version is submitted for you. If you press Submit and then change your text, press Submit again.
- **Stay within the word limit.** A draft over 500 words cannot be submitted, not even at the deadline. An
  advice memo under 150 words is submitted at the deadline but earns no fee.

## Tips

- Fill in your **profile** early: companies choose a firm from it.
- Your Home page lists every offer to answer and every memo to write, with countdowns.
- Read each client's crisis card and plan, and give advice with numbers that fit them. The cards are sealed while
  hiring is open, so you are selling your judgement, not a ready answer: win the work first, then read the card.
- **One screen per team.** Extra phones or laptops still work, but they update more slowly.
- The **Leaderboard** shows every company's **PRIMUS Rating** (it starts at 1,000 and moves with each released
  score against the other companies of the same sector): a quick way to see which clients are doing well.
