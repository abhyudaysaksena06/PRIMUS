# The craftsmanship shutdown

Luxury · Round 2 crisis card · Operations · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

A fire shuts the company's only production atelier, delaying 35% of the coming collection; VIP clients have already pre-ordered. Twist: a mass-production manufacturer offers to fulfil the orders in half the time, which could compromise craftsmanship and exclusivity. The client must decide to outsource, delay, compensate customers, or a defensible combination, and what it tells its VIP clients.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Makes the card's decision and prices it (refunds, compensation, lost sales, risk to the brand).
- Says what VIP clients are told, by whom, when, and what choice they are offered.
- Explains how exclusivity and craft standards are protected whatever is decided.
- Covers restoring production capacity and reliance on a single atelier.
- States how success is measured (pre-orders kept, cost of compensation, prices and exclusivity held).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- Ignores the VIP clients who have already ordered.
- Treats the mass-production offer as free of brand risk, or dismisses it without weighing the delay.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
