# The luxury market crash

Luxury · Round 2 crisis card · Money · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

A sudden downturn cuts discretionary spending: luxury retail falls 18%, high-end hospitality bookings 25% and luxury real estate deals 30%. The next funding round is postponed. Twist: the largest investor asks for a 20% price cut to keep sales up; the brand team says that would end the brand. The client must decide to discount, hold prices and cut costs, or launch something cheaper under another name, and show the runway.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Makes the card's decision and explains the trade-off between sales now and brand value later.
- Shows the cash runway in months under the card's falls and the chosen plan.
- Weighs cost cuts or new revenue by their effect on the brand, and names revenue that holds up in a downturn.
- Covers how the largest investor is answered and how top clients are kept.
- States how success is measured (months of runway, prices held or not, top clients kept).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- No runway figure, although the card asks for one.
- Ignores the investor's demand in the twist.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
