# The Ambassador Scandal

Luxury · Round 2 crisis card · Trust · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

The fashion house signed a five-year, ₹100 crore endorsement deal with a global celebrity, who is then exposed in a major public controversy. Within 48 hours sentiment drops 30%, three major retail partners demand an explanation, pre-orders fall 20%, and customers link the brand with the controversy. Twist: the celebrity owns 15% of the company, and ending the endorsement could trigger a contractual dispute and a buyback of their shares at a substantial premium. The client must decide to terminate now, retain and defend, or suspend the campaign pending an independent investigation.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Makes the card's decision and prices it (buyback premium, legal costs, contract terms, lost sales and partner risk).
- Says what is communicated in the first 48 hours, and that it is true and consistent with the brand's values.
- Covers how retail partners and clients are reassured directly.
- Addresses dependence on a single face in future and the 15% stake.
- States how success is measured (pre-orders and sentiment over the coming weeks, cost of the option, partners retained).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- Ignores the 15% shareholding and the buyback risk.
- Offers a press statement with no decision and no cost.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
