# Reengineering the next era of luxury

Luxury · Round 1 problem card · judged as a PRD (PITCH)

## The case in brief

The luxury market is moving beyond ownership, exclusivity and status towards hyper-personalisation, intelligent services and immersive experiences. Gaps remain in how luxury is conceived, delivered, accessed and sustained: fragmented client journeys, underdeveloped experiences, inefficient service and changing expectations of affluent consumers. Teams design a startup that uses new business models, technology or unconventional value propositions to create the next generation of luxury products, services or experiences. The card gives no fixed numbers, so any figures are the team's own and should be plausible and internally consistent.

## What a strong answer addresses

- The client segment (for example new-money professionals, NRIs, the ultra-rich, aspirational buyers) and what they lack today, in numbers where possible.
- Why the offer is luxury (scarcity, craft, service or access), not just a higher price.
- How trust is earned: authentication, privacy, service quality.
- A first version with its core features named, and unit economics per member, item or experience.
- Success metrics such as members retained, spend per client, resale value kept or authentication accuracy, with a target.

## Common weak answers

- Generic advice or a list of trends with no specific user or problem, or restating the card.
- No first version, no numbers (or implausible ones) and no way to measure success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
