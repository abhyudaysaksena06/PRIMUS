# The next-generation exodus

Luxury · Round 2 crisis card · Market · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

Younger affluent consumers are leaving traditional luxury brands for independent designers and niche labels; the company's sales to customers under 35 fall 25%. Twist: a popular independent designer offers a collaboration but demands complete creative freedom and equal brand recognition. The client must decide to collaborate on those terms, acquire the label, or reinvent its own brand for the new generation, and how it keeps its existing clients.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Explains why younger buyers are leaving: what the niche labels offer that the brand does not.
- Makes the card's decision with its cost, the control it keeps or gives up, and its risk to the brand.
- Says how existing clients are kept while new ones are courted.
- Sizes the loss (25% of under-35 sales) and sets a target.
- States how success is measured (under-35 sales, price premium, older clients retained).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- Says 'go digital' or 'use influencers' without linking it to the decision or the numbers.
- Ignores the designer's terms in the twist.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
