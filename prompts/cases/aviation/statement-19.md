# First-time flyers who fly with confidence

Aviation · Round 3 problem statement 19 · judged as the company's solution (SOLUTION) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

- **Client:** Women and elderly travellers in a district with a new airport.
- **The numbers:** 7 in 10 of the district's travellers have never flown; 1 in 4 who books cancels before flying.
- **The catch:** Fear of the unknown, language, ID rules and being alone at the airport.
- **Success, as the client defines it:** Cancellations before flying halved, with 8 in 10 saying they would fly again.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Each step of the journey where first-time flyers drop out (booking, ID, reaching the airport, boarding).
- The help offered at each step for women and elderly travellers, including language and travelling alone.
- Who pays, and how halving cancellations (1 in 4 today) and the '8 in 10 would fly again' target are measured.
- Delivery (who does what, first step, cost) and a measure of success against the target, with a date.

## Common weak answers

- Generic advice ('use AI', 'build an app') with no mechanism, or restating the brief.
- Ignoring the catch, which is the main constraint.
- No numbers, no link from the plan to the target, or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
