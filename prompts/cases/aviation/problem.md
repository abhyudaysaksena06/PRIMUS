# The Empty Runway: making small-city air travel work

Aviation · Round 1 problem card · judged as a PRD (PITCH)

## The case in brief

Small airports in India sit underused and many regional routes shut when their support ends: airlines cannot fill small aircraft, first-time flyers find flying unfamiliar and costly, cargo space goes unused and airports earn too little. Teams design a startup that turns a small airport into a lasting, self-sustaining link, creating measurable value for one of five payers: the traveller, a regional airline, an airport operator, a shipper, or the state or district government. Fact sheet (the same for every team): 120 small airports and 380 subsidised routes; 64 turboprops of 72 seats run by 4 operators; 58% average seat load; average fare ₹3,400; up to 800 kg of cargo space a flight; airports 38 km from town on average; 1 airport in 3 loses 40 or more days a year to fog; each route's subsidy runs for 3 years. Every PRD must include one line on scaling from one airport to many.

## What a strong answer addresses

- A named payer, how many of them there are, and what the problem costs them today, worked out from the fact sheet.
- A first version that can be tested at one airport, route or district.
- Unit economics per seat, per kg, per passenger or per airport, consistent with the fact sheet.
- The required line on how the business scales from one runway to many.
- Success metrics such as seat load, cancellation rate, door-to-gate minutes, ₹ per kg or airport income beyond flights.
- Numbers that do not contradict the fact sheet; the card says contradictions are marked down. The launch airport's name is only a label.

## Common weak answers

- Generic advice or a list of trends with no specific user or problem, or restating the card.
- No first version, no numbers (or implausible ones) and no way to measure success.
- Numbers that contradict the fact sheet, or no line on scaling to many airports.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
