# Near-miss in the fog

Aviation · Round 2 crisis card · Trust · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

A turboprop lands in 40 m visibility at a small airport; no one is hurt but the cockpit audio goes viral. The regulator finds 40 of the 120 airports lack working landing aids and suspends low-visibility flying there for 60 days. First-time-flyer bookings fall 30% across the network. Twist: the regulator will name the 40 airports only in 10 days, and users ask daily whether their airport is safe. The client must decide what it tells users before the list, whether it pauses sales at airports it suspects, and how it wins back first-time flyers once the list is out.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Says what users are told before the list, separating honestly what is known from what is not.
- Makes the twist's decision on pausing sales, with reasons and the cost of over-warning and of under-warning.
- Gives users at affected airports real alternatives.
- Plans how first-time flyers are won back after the list, with the cost.
- States how success is measured (bookings over the 60 days, whether its statements prove right, how fast first-time flyers return).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- Claims to know which airports are safe before the regulator does.
- Offers reassurance only, with no decision on sales and no cost.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
