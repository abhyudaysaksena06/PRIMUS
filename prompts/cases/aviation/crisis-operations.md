# Grounded at Dawn

Aviation · Round 2 crisis card · Operations · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

An engine-inspection order grounds 22 of the 64 turboprops for 21 days; 131 of the 380 routes stop overnight, and in week one 27,000 booked passengers and 92 tonnes of booked cargo are stranded. Twist: operators will restore only routes that show committed demand, and want a 14-day list of confirmed bookings from partners by Friday; routes without one may be dropped for good. The client must decide what it does for its users in the first 72 hours, whether it moves them to road, rail or another airline and who pays, and how it helps its route prove demand by Friday.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Covers the first 72 hours for stranded users: rebooking, transfer or refund, and who pays.
- Responds to the twist: whether and how a committed-demand list is built by Friday, or why not.
- Keeps earning where routes still fly, and costs the 21 days against revenue lost.
- Stays on the client's own business and payer, not the whole industry.
- States how success is measured (users kept, cost of looking after them, the route restored).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- Addresses neither the 72-hour response nor the Friday deadline.
- Ignores who pays for transfers or refunds.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
