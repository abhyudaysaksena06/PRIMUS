# The subsidy cliff

Aviation · Round 2 crisis card · Money · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

The Centre cuts route support: subsidy on 150 of the 380 routes ends in 90 days, a year early, and operators say 90 of them will close unless fares rise 40%. States will pay all startup invoices 90 days late, and the next funding round is postponed. Twist: one state offers to keep its 40 routes flying for 2 more years if aviation startups pay ₹25 lakh each into a shared route fund, returned only if those routes reach 65% seat load within a year. The client must decide to pay in, stay out, or cut costs and wait, and show its cash runway under that choice.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Makes the twist's decision and prices it: ₹25 lakh at risk against what those 40 routes bring the company.
- Shows the cash runway in months under the chosen plan, with state payments 90 days late.
- Covers protecting cash (costs, collections, terms) and dependence on subsidised routes or state payers.
- Uses the card's numbers consistently.
- States how success is measured (months of runway, share of revenue tied to subsidy or state payers).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- No runway figure, although the card asks for one.
- Ignores the 90-day payment delay or the postponed round.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
