# The giant comes to town

Aviation · Round 2 crisis card · Market · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

The national airline with 61% market share takes 70 of the 140 best regional routes next quarter, flying 78-seaters at a ₹1,999 launch fare and bundling its own app, cab, cargo and maintenance. Regional bookings on those routes fall 34% in a week and 2 of the 4 operators may exit. Twist: the giant offers every startup a 3-year exclusive contract on its routes only: volume triples, the unit price falls 40%, and the giant owns the customer. The client must decide to sign, refuse or negotiate, which customers it locks in before the switch, and whether it competes on the giant's routes or moves to the others.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Makes the twist's decision (sign, refuse or negotiate) and compares the options in revenue terms over the 3 years (three times the volume at 40% less per unit is about 1.8 times the revenue, with no customer of its own).
- Identifies which customers are most likely to stay and how they are kept.
- Says where the company competes (the giant's routes or the others) and why.
- Shows the margin after any price response.
- States how success is measured (customers kept, revenue per customer, dependence on the giant).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- Ignores the exclusivity offer or decides without numbers.
- Assumes the company can simply undercut a ₹1,999 fare at no cost.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
