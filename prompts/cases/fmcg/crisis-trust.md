# Fake sustainability data

FMCG · Round 2 crisis card · Trust · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

A journalist shows that a key supplier faked the 'plastic-free' and 'organic' data printed on the company's packs, and #Greenwashing trends with the company's name in it. Twist: a second supplier may also be faking data, but checking takes 6 weeks. The client must decide whether to speak now or wait until it is sure, and who is told, and when.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Makes the disclosure decision the card asks for (now or after checking), with reasons, including what is said about the second supplier during the 6 weeks.
- Says who is told (shoppers, retailers, regulators, investors) and in what order.
- Deals with packs already carrying false claims and estimates the cost and the sales at risk.
- Explains how supplier claims will be verified from now on, and how proof is shown.
- States how success is measured (speed of response, retailer reaction, cost of relabelling and audits, trust indicators).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- Promises transparency without saying what will be disclosed, to whom and when.
- Ignores the second supplier and the 6-week check.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
