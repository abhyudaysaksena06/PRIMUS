# The product nobody wants to sell

FMCG · Round 2 crisis card · Operations · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

A low-priced personal-care product is in demand across the country and consumers prefer it, but big retail chains earn less per metre of shelf from it and are delisting it. Twist: the biggest competitor pays retailers to remove it, while a major quick-commerce app offers exclusivity with guaranteed nationwide visibility, on condition that the app controls customer access, pricing and distribution. The client must choose between fighting for traditional retail, going direct to consumers, accepting the exclusivity, or a defensible combination, and must say what it gives up.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Makes the decision the card asks for and states plainly what the company gives up (margin, reach or control).
- Puts numbers on the routes it considers: margin offered to retailers, cost and time of direct sales, the value and risk of the platform's terms.
- Keeps today's buyers supplied while any change of channel happens.
- Addresses dependence on a single channel or platform in future.
- Names how success is measured (stores still stocking, margin after the chosen route, share of revenue from one channel).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- Picks a route without weighing what it costs or what is given up.
- Ignores the competitor's incentives or the exclusivity terms in the twist.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
