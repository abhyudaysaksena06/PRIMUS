# The product that outgrew its market

FMCG · Round 2 crisis card · Money · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

A packaged snack went viral and demand rose 300% in six weeks; the company expanded production, bought machinery and signed distribution agreements. The trend faded, leaving ₹12 crore of unsold stock, three-year manufacturing commitments, costly idle capacity and retailers demanding compensation. Twist: a major international retailer offers to buy all remaining stock at 40% below the original wholesale price, which could reset what consumers expect to pay. The client must choose to liquidate, protect the brand and sell slowly, reinvent the product, or a defensible combination.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Makes the card's decision and explains the trade-off between cash now and pricing power later.
- Puts numbers on the options: cash recovered (the offer is worth about 60% of wholesale on ₹12 crore, before costs), write-offs, expiry and storage, effect on future prices.
- Deals with the three-year commitments, the idle capacity and the retailers' compensation demands.
- Says what changes in forecasting or contracts so that it does not happen again.
- States how success is measured (cash recovered and how fast, write-offs, home-market prices held).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- Discusses only marketing ideas and ignores the stock, the commitments and the cash.
- Accepts or rejects the offer without valuing it.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
