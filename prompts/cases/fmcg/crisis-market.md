# The secret ingredient

FMCG · Round 2 crisis card · Market · judged as the company's crisis plan (CRISIS) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

The flagship beverage's formula is the company's main advantage. The chief product developer, who had the complete formula, joins a competitor, which launches a near-copy at 30% lower prices; sales fall as retailers give it shelf space. Twist: the supplier of the drink's most distinctive ingredient has signed an exclusive deal with the competitor. The client must decide between legal action, a new formula, acquiring or licensing from the competitor, or a defensible combination.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Deals with supply (how the drink keeps being made without the exclusive ingredient) as well as competition (a cheaper copy).
- Makes the card's decision, with its cost and a realistic timeline.
- Says how the brand holds price and shelf space while the decision plays out.
- Covers how formulas, staff contracts and supplier terms are protected in future.
- States how success is measured (market share, supply secured, cost against revenue protected).

Any of the card's options, or a reasoned combination, can score highly if it is justified with the card's numbers and fits the client's own business (its PRD).

## Common weak answers

- Treats it only as a legal or only as a marketing problem and ignores the ingredient supply.
- Assumes a lawsuit or a new recipe works instantly, with no cost or time.
- Generic advice that could fit any card, or restating the card.
- No decision, no numbers (or numbers that contradict the card), or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
