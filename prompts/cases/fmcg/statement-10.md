# Personalised nutrition for India's diabetic population

FMCG · Round 3 problem statement 10 · judged as the company's solution (SOLUTION) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

- **Client:** Some of India's 10 crore people with diabetes, and their families.
- **The numbers:** Most see a doctor twice a year and get the same generic diet chart.
- **The catch:** Food is cultural, budgets are tight, and nobody wants to feel ill.
- **Success, as the client defines it:** Members whose blood sugar improves measurably within 6 months, at under Rs 500 a month.

## What a strong answer addresses

- Who the users are and how advice or food is personalised beyond a generic diet chart.
- How it respects food culture, tight budgets and the wish not to feel ill.
- How blood-sugar improvement within 6 months is measured, and the cost against under Rs 500 a month, and who pays.
- Delivery (who does what, first step, cost) and a measure of success against the target, with a date.

## Common weak answers

- Generic advice ('use AI', 'build an app') with no mechanism, or restating the brief.
- Ignoring the catch, which is the main constraint.
- No numbers, no link from the plan to the target, or no measure of success.
