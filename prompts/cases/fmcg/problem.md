# Fast-moving consumer goods

FMCG · Round 1 problem card · judged as a PRD (PITCH)

## The case in brief

The client is the FMCG industry: consumers want healthier products, transparency, sustainability, personalisation and products that are always available, while companies struggle to read changing preferences, run supply chains, cut wastage, deliver the last mile and build trust. The results are failed launches, inventory inefficiency, lost sales and falling loyalty. Teams design a startup that tackles one or more of these challenges and creates measurable value for both businesses and end consumers. The card gives no fixed numbers, so any figures are the team's own and should be plausible and internally consistent.

## What a strong answer addresses

- A specific user (a brand, distributor, shop owner or shopper) and the problem in numbers: how many have it and what it costs them.
- A first version small enough to launch in one city or one category, with its core features named.
- Unit economics: cost and margin per unit, order or shop, and who pays.
- How it reaches shops or shoppers, and how it scales beyond the first version.
- Success metrics such as waste %, fill rate, repeat purchase or margin, with a target.
- Value for both the business and the end consumer, as the card asks.

## Common weak answers

- Generic advice or a list of trends with no specific user or problem, or restating the card.
- No first version, no numbers (or implausible ones) and no way to measure success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
