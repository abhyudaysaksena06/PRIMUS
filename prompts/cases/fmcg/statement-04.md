# Honest health labels shoppers actually read

FMCG · Round 3 problem statement 4 · judged as the company's solution (SOLUTION) or its hired firm's advice memo (ADVICE)

## The case in brief

- **Client:** Shoppers who spend 4 seconds choosing a pack of noodles.
- **The numbers:** Under 10% read the label, and most claims on the front are vague.
- **The catch:** Brands fear honest labels will hurt sales.
- **Success, as the client defines it:** A label that changes what at least 1 shopper in 5 buys, with sales held.

## What a strong answer addresses

- The label or tool and how claims on it are kept honest.
- How a shopper with 4 seconds notices and understands it (under 10% read labels today).
- How brands are persuaded despite their fear of lost sales, and how '1 shopper in 5 changes what they buy, with sales held' is measured.
- Delivery (who does what, first step, cost) and a measure of success against the target, with a date.

## Common weak answers

- Generic advice ('use AI', 'build an app') with no mechanism, or restating the brief.
- Ignoring the catch, which is the main constraint.
- No numbers, no link from the plan to the target, or no measure of success.

## Fairness reminders

- Score substance, not length, jargon, polish or a confident tone. Give no credit for famous brand or people's names and no penalty for simple language or non-native English.
- Judge only what is written. Several different approaches can all score highly.
- The rubric decides the score; this brief only gives context.
